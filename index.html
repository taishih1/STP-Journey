Training & Tips

What I learned from 4 years of STP rides

Our Training

🚲

We trained for 4 months leading up to the ride. Every weekend we hit the road, starting with shorter distances and increasing mileage week by week until we were ready for the full 200.

Tips for Your Ride

💧

Stay Hydrated

Drink water consistently throughout the ride. Don't wait until you're thirsty - by then it's too late.

🐢

Pace Yourself

It's not a race. Start slower than you think you need to. Save energy for the second half.

🍴

Eat at Every Stop

Fuel up at rest stops even if you're not hungry. Your body needs calories to keep going.

☀

Sunscreen & Layers

Weather changes fast. Apply sunscreen and bring layers for morning chill and afternoon heat.

🛠

Check Your Bike

Get your bike tuned up before the ride. Check tires, brakes, and gears. Bring a spare tube.

💪

Mind Over Miles

When your legs say quit, your mind keeps you going. Focus on the next rest stop, not the finish line.

My Years

A journey through time - 2022 to 2025

2022

The Beginning

My first STP ever! I was 12 years old and decided to take on the 206-mile challenge. I didn't know what to expect, but I finished it!

2023

Building Momentum

Year three of STP! By now, this ride had become a tradition. I was getting faster, stronger, and more confident on the bike.

2024

One Day Challenge

I completed STP in ONE DAY with my mom - 206 miles from sunrise to sunset. The hardest thing I've ever done!

2025

Becoming an Ambassador

As an STP Ambassador, I rode with my 10-year-old sister and dad, passing on the tradition to the next generation.

My Story

How It Started

I started biking STP in 2022, when I was 12 years old. It was honestly just a random challenge I thought of. I don't remember much about my first ride, but I do remember finishing!

Getting the Family Involved

I've done STP every year since that first ride, and now I've gotten my whole family to join me. Last year, I did STP in one day with my mom - which was pretty hard. 206 miles in a single day is no joke.

Becoming an Ambassador

This year was different. I was an STP Ambassador, so I rode at a slower pace to support other riders by handing out food and water. I was mostly riding with my 10-year-old sister and my dad.

Looking Ahead

I was kinda unsuccessful as an ambassador - not many people took my help. But that won't stop me from trying again in 2026.
